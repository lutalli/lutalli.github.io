title: Linear Mapping
updated: 2026-05-11

> {{ "LM#DEF:Linear Mapping" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be [vector spaces](/codex/VS) over a [field](/codex/FLD) $F$. A [mapping](/codex/F) $f:V\to W$ is **linear** if:
>
> {:.par-upper-roman}
> 1. $f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y)$ for all $x$, $y\in V$ (*additivity*).
> 2. $f(\lambda x) = \lambda f(x)$ for all $x\in V$ and $\lambda\in F$ (*homogeneity*).

- Synonym of *linear mapping*: **vector space homomorphism**

> {{ "LM#DEF-KER:Kernel" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ vector spaces over a field $F$ and $f:V\to W$ be a linear mapping. The **kernel** of $f$ is
>
> $$ \ker f = f^{-1}[\{0\}]. $$

---

> {{ "LM#PROP-TST" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$ and $f:V\to W$. $f$ is linear if
>
> $$ f(\lambda x + y) = \lambda f(x) + f(y) $$
>
> for all $x$, $y\in V$ and $\lambda \in F$.

> {{ "LM#PROP-A" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces. For any linear mapping $f:V\to W$:
>
> {:.par-upper-roman}
> 1. $f(0) = 0$.
> 2. $f(x - y) = f(x) - f(y)$ for all $x$, $y\in V$.

> {{ "LM#PROP-LI" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$ and $f:V\to W$ be a linear mapping. For any [linear dependent](/codex/LI) $S\subseteq V$, $f[S]$ is linear dependent.

> {{ "LM#PROP-S" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$ and $X\subseteq V$, $Y\subseteq W$ be [subspaces](/codex/SVS). For any linear mapping $f:V\to W$, $f[X]$ and $f^{-1}[Y]$ are subspaces.

> {{ "LM#PROP-DIM" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$. For any linear mapping $f:V\to W$:
>
> {:.par-upper-roman}
> 1. $\dim\im f \leq \dim V$.
> 2. If $f$ is an [isomorphism](/codex/MOR), then $\dim V = \dim W$.

---

> {{ "LM#PROP-HOM" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$. $\hom(V,W)$ is a subspace of $\map(V,W)$.

> {{ "LM#PROP-END" | cod }}
>
> For any vector space $V$, $(\endo V,\circ,+)$ is a [ring](/codex/RING).

---

> {{ "LM#PROP-K" | cod }}
>
> Let $V$, $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$. For any linear mapping $f:V\to W$:
>
> {:.par-upper-roman}
> 1. $\im f$ and $\ker f$ are subspaces.
> 2. $f$ is an isomorphism if and only if $\ker f=\\{0\\}$.
> 3. If $f$ is an isomorphism, then for any linear independent $S\subseteq V$, $f[S]$ is linear independent.

---

## Notation

$\map(X,Y)$
: The set of all mappings from $X$ to $Y$.
